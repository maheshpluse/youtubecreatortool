The quick answer: a YouTube Short can be up to three minutes long, as long as it has a square or vertical aspect ratio. YouTube extended the limit from 60 seconds to three minutes in October 2024, and that change had knock-on effects on how Shorts are classified, how music and Content ID work, and which length actually performs best.

This guide covers the current rules in detail, explains what happens to videos that fall just outside them, and — more usefully — helps you decide how long your Shorts should be. Maximum length and ideal length are very different things, and choosing the right one for each idea is one of the easiest ways to improve Shorts performance.

The current Shorts length rules

According to YouTube's help page on uploading YouTube Shorts, to be treated as a Short, a video must:

  • be three minutes (180 seconds) or shorter, and
  • have a square or vertical aspect ratio (for example 1:1 or 9:16).

Vertical 9:16 at 1080 × 1920 pixels is the standard recommended format, because it fills a phone screen in the Shorts player. There is no minimum length beyond what makes sense for your content, though extremely short clips of a second or two rarely perform well.

Check the latest rules

YouTube updates Shorts features regularly. The rules in this article reflect YouTube's documentation at the time of writing; always check the official help page before building a strategy around a specific limit.

How YouTube decides a video is a Short

Upload a video that meets both conditions — up to three minutes, square or vertical — and YouTube automatically classifies it as a Short. You do not need to add #Shorts to the title or description, although some creators still do. The video then appears in the Shorts feed, on the Shorts tab of your channel, and under Shorts in your analytics.

Videos that do not meet the conditions are classified as regular long-form videos:

  • A vertical video of 3 minutes and 5 seconds is a long-form video, not a Short.
  • A horizontal 16:9 video of 45 seconds is a long-form video, not a Short.

Long-form videos are shown on the watch page, in search, on the home feed and in suggested videos, and they are monetized with ads attached to the video. Shorts are monetized through the Shorts revenue-sharing model. Getting the classification right therefore matters for both discovery and earnings.

How the limit has changed

When Shorts launched, the maximum length was 60 seconds. In October 2024, YouTube raised it to three minutes for videos with a square or taller aspect ratio, as announced on the YouTube official blog. Videos uploaded before the change were not reclassified; longer vertical videos uploaded before the change remain long-form videos.

The change gave creators room for stories, tutorials and explanations that did not fit into a minute, and aligned YouTube with longer short-form limits on other platforms. It did not change the fact that shorter Shorts often perform better — more on that below.

Caveats for three-minute Shorts

Content ID and copyright

YouTube's help documentation notes that Shorts longer than one minute with an active Content ID claim may be blocked globally. In practice, this means you should be especially careful with copyrighted music or clips in longer Shorts. Use only audio you have rights to, or music from the YouTube Audio Library or the Shorts audio picker where licensing allows.

Music clip lengths

When you add music from YouTube's Shorts audio library, the available clip length may be limited, depending on the track. If your Short is longer than the music, plan the audio accordingly.

Remixing

Some remix features may be limited for longer Shorts. If remix-driven discovery matters to your strategy, check the current behaviour. Our guide to the Shorts remix feature covers how remixing works.

Shorts templates and editing tools

Some in-app editing features have been updated for the longer limit, but availability can vary by device and region. If a feature is missing, check that your YouTube app is up to date.

Maximum length vs ideal length

Just because you can make a three-minute Short does not mean you should. In the Shorts feed, viewers decide within a second or two whether to keep watching or swipe. Every extra second is another chance for them to swipe away.

Two metrics matter most for Shorts discovery:

  • Viewed vs swiped away — the percentage of people who chose to watch rather than scroll past. Our guide to improving the Shorts view-to-swipe ratio covers this.
  • Average percentage viewed — how much of the Short people watch. On Shorts this can exceed 100% because of loops.

A tight 25-second Short that people watch in full and rewatch will often be shown to more people than a 2-minute Short that most viewers leave halfway through. The general rule: make the Short as long as the idea needs, and not a second longer.

Finding your ideal length with analytics

Your audience is the only reliable guide. Use YouTube Studio to find your sweet spot:

  1. In Analytics, open Advanced mode and filter by content type: Shorts.
  2. Export or record, for each Short over the last 90 days: length, views, viewed vs swiped away, average percentage viewed and subscribers gained.
  3. Group Shorts into length buckets: under 20 seconds, 20–40, 40–60, 60–120, 120–180.
  4. Compare median results for each bucket, not the single best video.
  5. Check the retention graph on your longer Shorts. If most drop-off happens after a certain second, that is a natural cut point for future Shorts.

Making longer Shorts that hold attention

If your idea genuinely needs more than a minute, structure it so viewers stay:

  • Hook in the first second. Start with the most interesting moment, a bold statement or the result. No logos or introductions.
  • Promise the payoff. Tell viewers what they will get by watching to the end — "the third tip is the one most people miss."
  • Change something every few seconds. A new angle, text overlay, cut or visual keeps the eye engaged.
  • Use on-screen text. Many people watch without sound. Captions keep them following along.
  • Cut ruthlessly. Remove pauses, filler words and repeated points. A tight edit often turns a 90-second draft into a stronger 50-second Short.
  • End with a loop or a link. A seamless loop encourages rewatches; a related video link sends viewers to your long-form content.

Plan the structure before you film. The script generator can produce a tight outline, and the video ideas generator helps you find Short-sized topics in your niche.

Optimizing the title, description and hashtags

Shorts are discovered mainly through the Shorts feed, but titles and hashtags still help YouTube understand your content, and Shorts do appear in search. Keep titles short and clear, with the main keyword early — the title generator can suggest options. Add a few relevant hashtags using the hashtag generator. Write a brief description that explains the Short and, where relevant, points to a related long-form video. The description generator can help.

Shorts length and monetization

Shorts in the YouTube Partner Program earn from a pooled share of ad revenue from the Shorts feed, allocated by each eligible creator's share of Shorts views. Creators receive 45% of their allocation, as explained in YouTube's Shorts monetization policies. Length does not directly change how much each view earns, but it affects how many views you get and whether viewers go on to watch your long-form videos. Views on Shorts that use unlicensed third-party content, or that are unoriginal, may not count toward revenue.

Common questions about Shorts length

Can I edit a long-form video into a Short?

Yes. YouTube lets you create Shorts from segments of your own long-form videos, and you can also upload a separately edited vertical cut. A Short cut from a long video should still work on its own, with its own hook.

What happens if my Short is 3:01?

It will be treated as a regular long-form video. Trim it to 3:00 or less before uploading.

Can I upload a horizontal Short?

Horizontal videos are not classified as Shorts, whatever their length. Reframe to vertical or square.

Do longer Shorts get more views?

Not automatically. Longer Shorts can build deeper engagement if they hold attention, but many creators find shorter Shorts earn more views because more viewers watch them fully.

Should I still add #Shorts?

It is not required for classification. It does no harm if used naturally, but relevant topic hashtags are more useful.

A simple testing plan

For the next month, make each Shorts idea in two lengths when possible: a tight version and a fuller version. Publish them a few days apart with different hooks, then compare viewed vs swiped away and average percentage viewed after seven days. After eight to ten pairs, the pattern for your audience will be clear. Keep the length that consistently wins, and revisit the test every few months as your audience grows and changes.

How Shorts length fits into your channel strategy

Shorts and long-form videos serve different jobs. Shorts are excellent for reaching new viewers quickly and cheaply; long-form videos are where deeper relationships and higher ad revenue usually come from. The three-minute limit gives you a middle ground, but it is worth being deliberate about which job each Short is doing. A 20-second Short designed to be discovered and rewatched is a different product from a 2-minute Short designed to teach something complete.

Many creators use a simple rule: if the idea can be delivered in under 60 seconds, make it a tight Short; if it needs three minutes, ask whether it would be better as a full long-form video with room for chapters, examples and ads. Our guide to how Shorts and long-form are recommended separately explains why keeping the two formats distinct can help both perform.

Whatever length you choose, the fundamentals do not change: a strong first second, a clear promise, relentless editing and a reason to watch again. Get those right, and the length becomes the easy part.

Technical checklist before uploading a Short

  • Length: 3:00 or shorter. Check the exact duration in your editor's export settings, not just the timeline.
  • Aspect ratio: 9:16 vertical (1080 × 1920) or 1:1 square. Avoid black bars that make a horizontal video look vertical.
  • Safe zones: keep key text and faces away from the bottom and right edges, where the title, channel name and buttons overlay the video.
  • Audio: only music and sound you have rights to use, especially on Shorts longer than a minute.
  • Captions: burned-in or auto-generated captions for viewers watching without sound.
  • First frame: something visually interesting from the very first frame, because there is no thumbnail click in the feed.
  • Related video: link a relevant long-form video where the feature is available, to guide interested viewers deeper into your channel.

Running through this list takes a minute and prevents the most common Shorts problems: videos misclassified as long-form, important text hidden behind buttons, and audio that triggers copyright claims.
