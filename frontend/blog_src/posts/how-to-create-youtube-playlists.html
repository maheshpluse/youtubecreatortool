Playlists are one of the most underrated tools on YouTube. They organise your channel, keep viewers watching one video after another, give your channel homepage structure, and can appear in search results in their own right. Yet many creators never make one, or make a single "All videos" playlist and stop there.

This guide is a complete, practical walkthrough of creating and managing YouTube playlists: how to make one on desktop and mobile, every setting and what it does, collaborative playlists, the difference between public, unlisted and private, how to optimize titles and descriptions for search, and how to organise playlists so they actually increase watch time. Why playlists matter

  • Longer viewing sessions. When someone watches a video from a playlist, the next video in that playlist plays automatically. That keeps viewers on your content rather than someone else's.
  • Discovery. Playlists can appear in YouTube and Google search results, and they can surface in recommendations.
  • Channel organisation. Playlists power the sections of your channel homepage, making it easy for new visitors to find what they want.
  • Series and courses. Numbered playlists turn standalone videos into a structured learning path.
  • Curation. You can include other creators' videos to build useful resources for your audience.

YouTube Analytics even reports playlist performance separately, including views, watch time and average time in playlist, so you can see which playlists keep people watching.

Creating a playlist on desktop

YouTube's help page on creating and managing playlists documents the current steps. There are two common ways to create one.

From a video

  1. Open the video you want to add.
  2. Click Save below the video (it may be under the three-dot "More" menu).
  3. Choose Create new playlist.
  4. Enter a title and choose the visibility: Public, Unlisted or Private.
  5. Click Create. The video is added, and the playlist now exists.

From YouTube Studio

  1. Open YouTube Studio.
  2. Select Content in the left menu, then the Playlists tab.
  3. Click New playlist (or Create > New playlist).
  4. Enter a title, description and visibility, and choose default ordering.
  5. Click Create, then add videos.

Creating playlists from Studio is better when you are building a planned structure for your own channel, because you can write the description and settings at the same time.

Creating a playlist on mobile

  1. Open the YouTube app and play the video you want to add.
  2. Tap Save below the video. The app may save to your most recent playlist automatically — tap Change in the confirmation to choose a different one.
  3. Tap New playlist.
  4. Enter a title, choose visibility, and tap Create.

You can also create and manage playlists from the You tab in the app, and from the YouTube Studio mobile app.

Managing playlists in YouTube Studio

Adding videos in bulk

In Studio's Content tab, tick the checkboxes next to several videos, then choose Add to playlist from the actions menu. This is the fastest way to organise an existing library.

Adding videos while uploading

In the upload flow's Details step, the Playlists dropdown lets you add the new video to one or more playlists before publishing. Make it a habit, so no video ends up orphaned.

Reordering

Open the playlist, then drag videos into the order you want. You can also sort automatically by date published, date added or popularity.

Removing videos

Open the playlist, click the three-dot menu next to a video and choose Remove from playlist. The video itself is not deleted.

Deleting a playlist

Open the playlist's settings and choose Delete playlist. The videos are not deleted, only the collection.

Public, unlisted or private?

  • Public playlists can appear in search, on your channel and in recommendations.
  • Unlisted playlists can be viewed by anyone with the link, but do not appear in search or on your channel. Useful for course materials, client deliverables or resources shared in an email.
  • Private playlists are visible only to you (and anyone you share with through permissions). Useful for planning or personal collections.

Note: an unlisted or private video inside a public playlist is still restricted by its own visibility setting.

Collaborative playlists

Collaboration lets other people add videos to your playlist. It is useful for community projects, co-hosted series, or a team curating resources.

  1. Open the playlist and select its settings or the collaborate option.
  2. Turn on Collaborators can add videos to this playlist.
  3. Copy the invitation link and share it with trusted people.
  4. You can remove collaborators or turn off collaboration at any time.

Because collaborators can add content to a playlist on your channel, only invite people you trust, and review additions regularly. Anything on your channel reflects on your brand and is expected to meet YouTube's Community Guidelines.

Optimizing playlists for search

Playlists can rank in YouTube search, especially for broad topics where viewers want several videos. Optimize them the same way you optimize videos.

Titles

Use the words people search for. "Beginner Guitar Lessons: Complete Course (Lessons 1–20)" is far better than "Guitar Stuff." Research phrasing with our keyword tool and generate variations with the title generator. YouTube allows up to 150 characters for playlist titles, but shorter is clearer.

Descriptions

Write a short, natural description that uses your main keyword and related terms, explains who the playlist is for, and says what they will be able to do after watching. Avoid keyword stuffing, which violates YouTube's spam policies. The description generator can create a first draft to edit.

The videos inside

A playlist is only as strong as its videos. Each should have its own optimized title, description and tags — use the SEO analyzer to check them. Include enough videos to make the playlist worth following; three is a minimum, five to twenty is common.

Playlist-optimized packaging

Consistent thumbnails across a series — the same layout, colours and numbered labels — make a playlist look like a complete, professional resource. Use the thumbnail ideas tool to design a template.

Organising playlists that increase watch time

1. Order by viewer journey

Put the best entry-point video first. For tutorials, start with the fundamentals. For entertainment series, start with the strongest episode, not necessarily the oldest.

2. Keep playlists focused

A playlist about one clear topic keeps viewers watching. A catch-all playlist of unrelated videos sends viewers away at the first mismatch.

3. Use series playlists for sequences

If you designate a playlist as an official series (available in some regions and settings), YouTube may show the next episode more prominently at the end of each video. Only use this for genuinely sequential content.

4. Link playlists everywhere

  • End screens: add a playlist element pointing to the relevant playlist. See our end screens and cards guide.
  • Cards: point to a playlist when you mention a related topic.
  • Descriptions and pinned comments: use playlist links rather than single-video links where appropriate.
  • Community posts: share a new playlist as a curated resource.

5. Feature playlists on your channel homepage

In Studio's Customization > Layout, add sections that show specific playlists. Order them by what new visitors are most likely to want. Our guide to optimizing your channel homepage layout walks through it.

6. Include other creators' videos — carefully

Curated playlists that include relevant videos from other creators can serve your audience well, especially where you have not yet covered a topic. Keep your own videos prominent, and only include content you would genuinely recommend.

7. Review with analytics

In YouTube Analytics, look at playlist views, watch time and average time in playlist. If viewers leave after the first video, reconsider the order or the second video's opening. Our guide to reading YouTube video statistics explains these reports.

Playlist ideas by channel type

  • Education: "Start here," beginner/intermediate/advanced paths, exam-topic playlists.
  • Reviews: by product category, by budget, "best of" roundups.
  • Cooking: by meal type, by cuisine, by cooking time, by diet.
  • Gaming: by game, by series, full walkthroughs in order.
  • Fitness: programmes by week, workouts by duration, by equipment.
  • Business: product tutorials, customer stories, webinars by topic.
  • Vlogs: by trip or location, by year, by series.

Common playlist mistakes

  • Never creating playlists. Every channel with more than ten videos benefits from at least three.
  • Vague titles like "Videos" or "Misc."
  • Mixing unrelated topics in one playlist.
  • Forgetting to add new uploads to relevant playlists.
  • Leaving deleted or private videos in public playlists, which creates gaps.
  • Making playlists but never linking to them from end screens, descriptions or the homepage.

Playlist checklist

  1. Group your existing videos into 3–8 focused topics.
  2. Create a public playlist for each, with a keyword-rich title and clear description.
  3. Order videos by viewer journey.
  4. Choose a strong playlist thumbnail.
  5. Add playlists to your channel homepage layout.
  6. Link playlists from end screens, cards and descriptions.
  7. Add every new upload to its playlists during the upload flow.
  8. Review playlist analytics monthly and adjust the order.

Twenty minutes spent organising your playlists today will keep paying off in watch time for as long as your videos are live.

Frequently asked questions

How many videos can a playlist hold?

YouTube playlists can hold thousands of videos, far more than most channels need. For viewers, focused playlists of five to fifty videos are usually easier to follow than enormous ones.

Can I add someone else's video to my playlist?

Yes, public videos from other channels can be added to your playlists, as long as the video's owner has not restricted it. You are linking to their video, not re-uploading it, so the views count for them.

Do playlist views count as video views?

Yes. When someone watches a video inside a playlist, the view counts for that video. Playlist analytics simply show how viewing happened through the playlist.

Can I make a playlist of Shorts?

Yes. Shorts can be added to playlists like any other video. Short-form playlists work well for tips series or themed collections, and they give viewers who enjoy one Short an easy way to watch more.

Why is my playlist not showing on my channel?

Check that the playlist is public, that it contains at least one public video, and that you have added a playlist section to your channel layout in Studio's Customization settings. Changes can take a short while to appear.

Playlists as a growth habit

The creators who get the most from playlists treat them as part of every upload rather than a one-time tidy-up. Before publishing any video, ask three questions: which playlist does this belong in, which playlist should its end screen point to, and does this video start a new series that needs its own playlist? Answering those questions every time takes seconds, and over a year it builds a channel where every video leads naturally to the next. That structure is exactly what turns a casual viewer who arrived from search into a subscriber who watches an hour of your content.
