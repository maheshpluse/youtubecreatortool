Most YouTube SEO advice is written for channels that already have authority. "Publish consistently and the algorithm will find you" is true for a channel with 200,000 subscribers and useless for one with 80, because the two are playing different games.

A small channel cannot win on authority, watch history or subscriber affinity. It wins on specificity — being the single best answer to a question narrow enough that bigger channels have not bothered. This checklist is built around that constraint.

The checklist

  1. Before you film: 6 checks
  2. While filming: 4 checks
  3. At upload: 9 checks
  4. After publishing: 5 checks
  5. Monthly: 3 checks
  6. What to ignore entirely

Before you film: 6 checks

1. The query is narrow enough to win. Search it. If the first six results all come from channels more than ten times your size with recent, thorough videos, pick a narrower variant. "Lighting setup" is unwinnable; "lighting setup for a small bedroom with one window" is not.

2. The demand is real. Confirm through YouTube's search suggestions, the Research tab in Studio, or Google Trends set to YouTube Search. Do not build on a phrase you invented.

3. You know the intent. Learn, decide, diagnose or understand — and the format follows from the answer. A tutorial answering a buying query satisfies nobody.

4. You have read the top result's comments. Complaints and unanswered questions there are the specification for a better video, in your audience's own vocabulary.

5. The title exists before the script. If you cannot write a compelling title, the idea is not sharp enough yet. Writing the title first also keeps the video on topic.

6. You know your differentiator. One sentence: why would someone watch yours instead of the existing top result? "It's more recent," "it assumes zero prior knowledge," "it covers the case they skipped" are all valid. "It's mine" is not.

While filming: 4 checks

7. Say the query out loud early. Naturally, in your opening sentence. It confirms to the viewer they are in the right place — which is the main job of the first ten seconds.

8. Deliver value before 0:30. No channel intro, no backstory, no "before we begin." A search viewer has a task and very little patience.

9. Structure in clear sections. These become your chapters, which improve navigation and let viewers jump to what they searched for.

10. Audio is clean. Bad audio destroys retention faster than any other technical fault, and retention is the strongest signal available to a small channel.

Where small channels actually win

You cannot out-authority a large channel, but you can out-specific them. The narrower and more genuinely useful the video, the less your subscriber count matters — because YouTube is trying to satisfy a query, not reward a follower count.

At upload: 9 checks

11. Title front-loads the query, keeps meaningful words inside roughly 60 characters, and adds a differentiator. No stuffed variants.

12. Thumbnail is designed against the competition. Open the search results, place yours among them, and confirm it stands out. Three or four words maximum, one focal point, readable at 20% size.

13. First 150 characters of the description genuinely summarise the video. This is what shows in search results and carries the most weight.

14. Chapters with timestamps. Starting at 0:00, at least three, with descriptive names.

15. Captions uploaded and corrected. Not just auto-generated. This gives the platform an accurate transcript, makes the video accessible to viewers who are deaf or hard of hearing, and improves watch time for sound-off viewing.

16. A handful of relevant tags. Ten to fifteen, focused. Tags are a minor signal — most useful for commonly misspelled topics — so spend two minutes here, not thirty.

17. Added to a relevant playlist, positioned as the logical next step from an existing video.

18. End screen points somewhere specific — the video that answers the next question, not a generic "watch more."

19. Self-certification completed honestly. It affects your ad suitability rating, and inaccurate answers erode trust in future ratings.

The two official guides worth an hour

How YouTube Search works explains what ranking actually depends on, and YouTube for Creators: getting started covers setup for new channels. Both beat most third-party SEO advice.

After publishing: 5 checks

20. Pin a comment that starts a conversation — a genuine question, not a promotion.

21. Reply to every comment for the first 48 hours. Early replies visibly signal that commenting is worthwhile and reliably lift total engagement.

22. Check the first-30-seconds retention curve at 48 hours. This is the single most useful diagnostic available. A steep early drop means the packaging promised something the video did not deliver.

23. Check CTR at day 7 against your channel average — and only once you have at least 1,000 impressions. Below that, the number is noise.

24. Check which queries it is appearing for. If it is ranking for a query you did not target, that is an opportunity: lean into the query it actually won.

Monthly: 3 checks

25. Re-optimise your five most-impressed videos. These already have distribution; small packaging improvements compound. Change one variable and wait fourteen days.

26. Update anything that has gone stale. Software changed, prices changed, a step no longer applies. Re-record the affected section rather than rebuilding the video, and note the update in the description.

27. Review returning viewers, not subscribers. Subscriber count only goes up and tells you little. Returning viewers tells you whether you are building an audience or just collecting traffic.

What to ignore entirely

  • Keyword-stuffed descriptions. Long lists of variants and hashtags add nothing and read badly to humans.
  • Tag generators and "SEO scores." Third-party scores are models of a system nobody outside YouTube can see.
  • Sub-for-sub and engagement pods. They pollute your audience data with people who will never watch, which actively harms distribution.
  • Buying views. A monetization risk and a data-integrity problem. Invalid traffic does not count toward thresholds.
  • Deleting underperforming videos. You lose watch hours, search rankings and long-tail traffic. Performance is judged per video, not on a channel average.
  • Posting-time obsession. Relevant for browse-driven content, irrelevant for the search-driven videos a small channel should be making.

Twenty-seven checks looks like a lot. In practice the pre-production six take fifteen minutes, the upload nine take ten, and everything else is a habit rather than a task. The compounding is the point: twenty narrow videos that each own a small query produce more reliable monthly traffic than one video that briefly touched a big one — and unlike a viral moment, that traffic is still arriving in two years.
