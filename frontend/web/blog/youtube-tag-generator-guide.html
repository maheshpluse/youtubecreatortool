YouTube Tag Generator Guide: How to Choose Tags That Help

By The VidSEOKit Editorial Team· September 19, 2026· 9 min read· 2,065 words

What YouTube tags really do, the limits and policies, five tag types, a step-by-step generation method and how tags compare with hashtags.

Search for "YouTube tag generator" and you will find dozens of tools that spit out forty tags for any keyword in a second. Many creators paste them in, hit publish and assume their SEO is done. Then the video gets a few hundred views and they wonder what went wrong.

The truth is that tags are a small part of YouTube SEO — YouTube says so itself — but they are not useless. Used well, they help with misspellings, variations and topic clarity. Used badly, they can clutter your metadata or even breach YouTube's spam policies. This guide explains what tags actually do, how to generate the right ones, how to use free tools like our tag extractor and keyword tool, and where your SEO effort should really go.

What YouTube tags actually do

YouTube's help page on adding tags to videos states that tags are descriptive keywords you can add to help viewers find your content, and that they play a minimal role in your video's discovery. It also notes that tags can be useful if your content's topic is commonly misspelled.

That official position matters. It means:

  • Tags will not rescue a video with a weak title, thumbnail or content.
  • Tags are most useful for misspellings, alternative spellings, abbreviations and synonyms that you would not naturally put in a title.
  • The title, thumbnail, description, captions and — above all — viewer behaviour matter far more.

Our guide to how the YouTube algorithm works explains the signals that carry more weight.

Tag rules and limits

  • Character limit: the tags field allows up to 500 characters in total. Commas between tags count, and tags containing spaces may count extra characters for the quotation marks added around them.
  • Where to add them: in YouTube Studio's video details, under Show more > Tags.
  • Visibility: tags are not shown on the watch page, but they can be read from the page source and by tools.
  • Policy: YouTube's spam, deceptive practices and scams policy prohibits excessive tags and misleading metadata. Adding irrelevant tags, or large numbers of tags, can lead to the video being removed.
Relevance over volume

Five to fifteen accurate tags are better than forty loosely related ones. Every tag should describe what is genuinely in the video.

The five types of tags to use

VariationsOther ways people phrase iteasy sourdough recipe, beginner sourdough

Some creators also add a channel-branded tag (their channel name) to help YouTube connect their videos. It does no harm, though its effect is small.

How to generate tags step by step

Step 1: Define the primary keyword

Choose the main phrase a viewer would search to find this video. It should also appear in your title and early in your description. Use our YouTube keyword tool to see related phrasings and pick the clearest one.

Step 2: Collect variations from autocomplete

Type your primary keyword into YouTube's search bar and note the suggestions. These show how real people phrase the topic. Pick the ones that genuinely match your video.

Step 3: Check what ranking videos use

Find the top few videos for your keyword and run them through our YouTube tag extractor. You will see which tags successful videos use. Do not copy them wholesale — use them as inspiration for relevant variations you missed, and ignore anything that does not describe your video.

Step 4: Add misspellings and alternatives

Think about how people actually type: joined or split words ("sour dough"), regional spellings ("color" vs "colour"), abbreviations, brand names with common errors. This is where tags provide the most value.

Step 5: Add broad and specific context

Add one or two broad category tags and two or three specific sub-topic tags that the video genuinely covers.

Step 6: Order and trim

Put the most important tags first. Remove duplicates, near-duplicates and anything that is not clearly relevant. Stay well within the character limit.

Step 7: Check the full package

Run your title, description and tags through the YouTube SEO analyzer to make sure the whole package is consistent and nothing important is missing.

Using tag generator and extractor tools well

Tag generators suggest keywords from a seed phrase. Extractors show the tags on existing videos. Both are helpful as idea sources, but neither replaces judgement.

Do:

  • Use generated tags as a list to choose from, not paste in full.
  • Keep only tags that accurately describe your video.
  • Compare extracted tags from several top videos to spot the common, relevant ones.
  • Combine tag research with keyword research for titles and descriptions.

Don't:

  • Paste 40 generated tags without reading them.
  • Copy a competitor's tags, especially branded tags with their channel name.
  • Add trending tags unrelated to your content.
  • Add other creators' names or brands to borrow their traffic — this is misleading metadata.

Tags vs hashtags

Tags and hashtags are different features:

TagsHashtags
Where addedTags field in StudioTitle or description, with #
Visible to viewersNoYes, as clickable links
PurposeHelp with misspellings and topic contextConnect videos to a clickable topic page
Limits500 characters totalMore than 60 hashtags means all are ignored; a few is best

YouTube's hashtag policies say that if a video has more than 60 hashtags, YouTube ignores all of them, and that misleading or excessive hashtags may lead to removal. In practice, two to five relevant hashtags work well. Our hashtag generator suggests relevant options.

Tag mistakes that hurt

  • Tag stuffing: filling all 500 characters with loosely related terms. It adds nothing and can breach spam policies.
  • Irrelevant trending tags: adding a trending topic that the video does not cover is misleading metadata.
  • Competitor names: using another creator's name or brand as a tag to appear next to their videos.
  • Repetition: ten near-identical tags ("how to bake bread," "how to bake bread easy," "how to bake bread at home easy") add little.
  • Relying on tags alone: spending ten minutes on tags and ten seconds on the title.
  • Mismatch: tags about one topic, title about another. Keep everything consistent.

Where tags fit in your SEO priorities

If you have limited time per video, spend it in roughly this order:

  1. Topic choice — something people search for and want to watch. Use the video ideas generator and keyword tool.
  2. Content quality and retention — a strong hook, clear structure and delivered promise. The script generator helps.
  3. Thumbnail — the biggest factor in click-through rate. Plan it with the thumbnail ideas tool.
  4. Title — clear keyword plus a reason to click. Try the title generator.
  5. Description — the keyword in the first sentences, a summary, chapters and links. Use the description generator.
  6. Captions — accurate captions improve accessibility and give YouTube more text to understand the video. See our guide to captions and SEO.
  7. Chapters, playlists and end screens — to extend viewing sessions.
  8. Tags and hashtags — the finishing touches.

A worked example

Video: a 12-minute tutorial on setting up a budget home office for video calls.

Title: Budget Home Office Setup for Video Calls (Under $200)

Tags: budget home office setup, home office setup for video calls, cheap home office setup, work from home setup, home office on a budget, video call lighting, webcam setup for zoom, home office ideas, desk setup budget, wfh setup

Hashtags: #HomeOffice #WorkFromHome #DeskSetup

Every tag describes something in the video. The list covers the primary keyword, variations, an abbreviation ("wfh"), and specific sub-topics (lighting, webcam). It is ten tags, well within the limit, and fully consistent with the title and content.

Updating tags on older videos

Revisiting old videos can be worthwhile, but prioritise the title, thumbnail and description first. When you do update tags, check YouTube Studio's search terms report for each video (Analytics > Reach > Traffic source: YouTube search). If viewers find the video using phrases you had not considered, and they accurately describe it, add them to the description and tags. Remove any tags that no longer fit. Change metadata carefully on videos that are already performing well, one element at a time.

Frequently asked questions

Do tags still matter on YouTube?

A little. YouTube says they play a minimal role in discovery, mostly helping with misspellings and variations. They are worth a minute per video, not an hour.

How many tags should I use?

There is no required number. Five to fifteen relevant tags is a practical range. Stop when you run out of accurate descriptions of the video.

Should my first tag be my main keyword?

It is a sensible habit to put your most important tag first, and it costs nothing. Consistency across title, description and tags matters more than the exact order.

Can I see other videos' tags?

Tags are not displayed on the watch page, but they can be viewed with tools. Our free tag extractor shows them for any public video.

Do tags work for Shorts?

You can add tags to Shorts the same way, but Shorts are discovered mainly through the Shorts feed, where tags have little influence. A clear title, relevant hashtags and a strong opening second matter much more.

Tags are the last five percent of YouTube SEO. Do them properly, keep them honest, and put the rest of your energy into the topic, the title, the thumbnail and a video worth watching to the end.

Tags for different content types

  • Tutorials: focus on the task and tool names, including version numbers and common misspellings of software names.
  • Reviews: include the product name, model number variations and "review," "vs" or "comparison" phrasings the video genuinely covers.
  • Gaming: include the game title and common abbreviations, the mode or level, and the type of video (guide, walkthrough, tips).
  • Vlogs: location names and their alternative spellings, plus the activity.
  • Non-English content: tags in the video's language, plus transliterated spellings people might type. Our multi-language growth guide covers translated metadata.

A two-minute tag routine

  1. Copy your primary keyword from the title.
  2. Add three to five variations from autocomplete or the keyword tool.
  3. Add any common misspellings or abbreviations.
  4. Glance at two top videos' tags in the tag extractor for anything relevant you missed.
  5. Add one or two broad category tags.
  6. Delete anything that does not accurately describe your video.

That routine takes about two minutes per upload and captures nearly all the value tags can offer. Spend the time you save on the thumbnail.

Why honest metadata protects your channel

Metadata — titles, thumbnails, descriptions, tags and hashtags — is how YouTube and viewers understand what a video offers. When it is accurate, the right viewers find the video, watch longer and are more satisfied, which helps the video reach more people like them. When it is misleading, viewers leave quickly, satisfaction signals drop, and the video may be removed under spam policies. Accurate tags are a small but real part of building a channel that YouTube can confidently recommend, and that viewers trust enough to return to.

Get the essentials right on every upload, keep your tags short and honest, and let the quality of your videos do the rest.
