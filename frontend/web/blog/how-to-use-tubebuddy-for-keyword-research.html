How to Use TubeBuddy for Keyword Research (Step-by-Step)

By The VidSEOKit Editorial Team· September 18, 2026· 5 min read· 1,158 words

Learn how to use TubeBuddy Keyword Explorer to find low-competition keywords, rank higher on YouTube, and grow your channel views faster.

Why YouTube Keyword Research is Non-Negotiable

YouTube is the world's second-largest search engine, handling over 3 billion searches every month. While high retention and engaging click-through rates (CTR) drive long-term algorithmic recommendations, strategic keyword research is the primary spark that gets new channels discovered.

If you upload videos without target keywords, you leave your discovery entirely to chance. By using TubeBuddy, a certified YouTube browser extension and optimization suite, you can uncover exactly what your target audience is searching for, quantify competition levels, and position your content to rank on search engine results pages (SERPs).

Setting Up TubeBuddy for Keyword Research

Before diving into keyword analysis, you need to configure the tool correctly:

  1. Install the Extension: Download the TubeBuddy browser extension for Google Chrome, Firefox, or Microsoft Edge.
  2. Link Your Channel: Sign in with your Google account associated with your YouTube channel and grant the necessary channel management permissions.
  3. Locate Keyword Explorer: Click the TubeBuddy menu icon (the red 'TB' logo) in the upper-right corner of YouTube, navigate to the Extension Tools section, and select Keyword Explorer.

Understanding TubeBuddy Keyword Metrics

When you enter a search term into TubeBuddy's Keyword Explorer, the tool generates a score from 1 to 100 alongside actionable insights. To make data-driven decisions, you must understand what these metrics mean.

1. Unweighted Score vs. Weighted Score

  • Unweighted Score: This score measures the keyword's overall viability across all of YouTube. It assumes an average, neutral channel. It is useful for general topic validation but doesn't factor in your channel's specific authority.
  • Weighted Score (Paid Feature): This is TubeBuddy's most valuable feature. It calculates a score customized specifically to your channel by comparing your average views, video output, and subscriber engagement against the creators currently ranking for that term.

2. Search Volume, Competition, and Optimization Strength

  • Search Volume: Represents how frequently users search for the query. High search volume indicates strong interest.
  • Competition: Measures how many videos are targeting this topic and how powerful those ranking channels are. Lower competition makes it easier to rank.
  • Optimization Strength: Shows how well current ranking videos have optimized their titles, tags, and descriptions for the search term. A poor optimization score among competitors creates an entry point for your channel.

Pro Tip: Aim for target phrases that receive an overall Weighted Score of 60/100 (Very Good) or higher, particularly when growing a small or medium-sized channel.

Step-by-Step Keyword Research Workflow

Step 1: Start with Broad Seed Keywords

Begin by typing a broad topic related to your niche into the search bar. For example, if you run a cooking channel, start with "air fryer recipes". TubeBuddy will immediately generate auto-complete suggestions and related search queries.

Step 2: Drill Down into Long-Tail Keywords

Broad keywords like "air fryer recipes" are dominated by established channels with millions of subscribers. To gain traction, focus on long-tail keywords—phrases containing 3 to 5+ words that target specific user intents.

  • Broad Seed Keyword: Air fryer recipes (High competition)
  • Long-Tail Keyword: Easy air fryer recipes for beginners (Medium competition)
  • Specific Long-Tail Keyword: Healthy low carb air fryer recipes for two (Low competition, high conversion)

Step 3: Analyze the Results & Competitor Tabs

Do not rely solely on the numerical score. Switch to the Results tab within Keyword Explorer to inspect top-ranking videos:

  • Top Channel Subscribers: Are the top 5 ranking channels massive channels (1M+ subs), or are smaller creators (under 10k subs) ranking too? If small channels rank, you can rank.
  • Title Alignment: Look at whether the exact target phrase appears at the beginning of top-ranking titles.
  • Video Age & View Counts: If top results were uploaded years ago and still gain views, the topic offers strong, evergreen search demand.

Step 4: Check Google Trends Data

TubeBuddy includes a integrated Google Trends tab. Verify whether interest in your target keyword is rising, stable, or declining over time before investing hours into video production.

How to Implement Keywords in Your Video Metadata

Once you selected your primary and secondary keywords, integrate them systematically across your video assets:

  1. Primary Keyword in Video Title: Place your main keyword within the first 60 characters of your title. Ensure it reads naturally to human viewers while remaining clear to algorithms.
  2. Description Optimization: Naturally place your primary keyword within the first 1 to 2 sentences of your video description. Write a detailed 200-300 word summary containing secondary long-tail keywords.
  3. Tags Strategy: Input your exact primary phrase as your very first tag. Fill out remaining tag slots with related broad and specific phrases provided by TubeBuddy’s Tag Suggestions feature.
  4. Spoken Keyword Mention: YouTube automatically transcribes audio. Speak your target keyword clearly during the introductory phase of your video.

Conclusion: Turn Search Data into Consistent Views

Mastering TubeBuddy for keyword research removes the guesswork from video topic creation. By leveraging weighted scores, identifying low-competition long-tail keywords, and properly optimizing your metadata, you can consistently capture targeted search traffic and establish a sustainable growth flywheel for your YouTube channel.

Reading the Keyword Score Correctly

TubeBuddy's keyword explorer combines search volume, competition and your own channel's ability to rank into one score. Treat it as a shortlisting tool rather than a verdict. A term with a modest score but a clear match to your existing audience often outperforms a high-scoring keyword that sits outside your niche.

A Repeatable Workflow

  1. List ten seed topics from your audience's questions and comments.
  2. Run each through the explorer and note related terms with low competition.
  3. Search the top terms on YouTube itself and check whether the top results are recent or dated.
  4. Pick one primary keyword and two supporting phrases per video.
  5. Place the primary phrase in the title, the first line of the description and spoken in the first minute.

Common Mistakes

Chasing only high volume is the most frequent error, since small channels rarely win those searches. Another is ignoring intent: a keyword can be popular yet attract viewers who want something different from your video. Always validate scores by looking at what actually ranks.

Frequently asked questions

Is TubeBuddy's Keyword Explorer free to use?

Yes, TubeBuddy offers a free browser extension that includes basic Keyword Explorer features limited to 3 searches per day. Upgrading to a paid plan unlocks unlimited searches and personalized Weighted Scores.

What is the difference between Weighted and Unweighted scores in TubeBuddy?

An Unweighted score evaluates keyword competitiveness for YouTube as a whole. A Weighted score tailors the competition and search metrics directly to your specific channel size, audience engagement, and view history.

Does TubeBuddy guarantee my video will rank on the first page?

No tool can guarantee top rankings because YouTube's algorithm heavily considers click-through rate (CTR) and audience retention. However, TubeBuddy drastically improves your chances by identifying low-competition, high-demand topics.

How does TubeBuddy compare to VidIQ for keyword research?

Both tools offer strong keyword tools, but TubeBuddy stands out with its Weighted scoring algorithm and seamless integration with Google Trends, while VidIQ focuses heavily on real-time competitor velocity tracking.
