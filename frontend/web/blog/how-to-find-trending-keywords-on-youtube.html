How to Find Trending Keywords on YouTube (Free Methods)

By The VidSEOKit Editorial Team· September 19, 2026· 9 min read· 2,078 words

Find trending YouTube keywords with Google Trends, autocomplete and the Research tab, validate them, produce fast, optimize, and avoid policy mistakes.

Advertisement

Trending keywords are the closest thing YouTube has to a head start. When interest in a topic is rising and few good videos exist, a well-packaged upload can be discovered by search and suggested traffic before the topic becomes crowded. Arrive a week late and the same video competes with hundreds of others.

This guide shows you how to find trending YouTube keywords using free sources, how to tell a genuine trend from a spike that will be gone in two days, how to decide whether a trend is right for your channel, and how to turn it into a video that ranks. It also covers the policy lines to stay on the right side of, because chasing trends is where many creators accidentally make misleading or reused content.

Three kinds of trending keywords

Not all trends behave the same way. Treat them differently:

TypeLifespanExampleStrategy
SpikeDaysA news event, viral momentOnly if you can publish within 24–48 hours and it fits your niche
SeasonalRecurs yearly"Tax filing tips," "back to school setup"Publish 2–4 weeks before the annual rise, update each year
Rising trendMonths to yearsA new tool, technique or hobby gaining adoptionBuild a series and aim to become the go-to channel

Rising trends are the most valuable for most channels. They combine growing demand with the chance to build lasting search traffic, rather than a one-off bump.

Free sources for finding trends

Google Trends, filtered to YouTube Search

Google Trends lets you switch the search type from web to YouTube Search. Enter a broad topic from your niche, set the time range to the past 90 days or 12 months, and look at Related queries sorted by Rising. Queries marked "Breakout" have grown very sharply. Compare terms side by side to see which is growing faster.

YouTube search autocomplete

Type the start of a topic in YouTube's search bar and note the suggestions. Autocomplete reflects what people are actually searching. Try adding letters after your topic ("budget camera a," "budget camera b") to uncover long-tail variations. New suggestions appearing where they did not before are a strong trend signal.

YouTube Studio Research tab

For channels with access, the Research tab in YouTube Studio's Analytics shows what your viewers and viewers across YouTube search for, including content gaps where searches are not well served by existing videos. YouTube explains it in its help page on the Research tab. Content gaps are especially valuable because demand exists and supply is thin.

The Trending and Explore pages

YouTube's trending and category pages show broad popular content. They lean towards entertainment and news, so they are more useful for spotting formats than keywords, but they are worth a weekly scan.

Your own comments and community

Questions that appear repeatedly in your comments, community posts and email are trends inside your niche. They are also the easiest to rank for, because your audience is already asking.

Look beyond YouTube

Many YouTube trends start elsewhere: product launch announcements, industry newsletters, forums and other social platforms. Following the primary sources in your niche often gives you days of warning before search volume rises.

Validating a trend before you film

Before investing hours in a video, run five checks.

  1. Is the curve still rising? In Google Trends, look at the last seven days. If interest has already peaked and is falling steeply, you are probably too late for a spike.
  2. Is YouTube demand real? Check the YouTube Search filter specifically. Some topics trend on the web but not on YouTube.
  3. What does the current results page look like? Search the keyword on YouTube. If the top results are old, low quality, or from small channels, there is room. If they are all fresh videos from large channels, a long-tail variation may be smarter.
  4. Is there search intent a video can satisfy? "How to set up X" and "X review" are video-friendly. A term people only search to find a login page is not.
  5. Will it still matter in three months? If yes, it is worth a thorough, evergreen treatment. If no, keep production light.

Deciding whether a trend fits your channel

A trend outside your niche can bring a burst of views from people who will never watch your usual videos. That can weaken the signals YouTube uses to recommend your channel to the right audience. Ask:

  • Would my current subscribers click this?
  • Can I add something my niche perspective uniquely offers?
  • Does it lead naturally to one of my existing videos?

If the answer to all three is no, skip it. There is always another trend, and consistency is worth more than one lucky upload. Our guide to how the YouTube algorithm works explains why audience match matters.

Producing fast without cutting quality

Trends reward speed, but low-effort content can be rejected by viewers and may conflict with YouTube's monetization policies on repetitive or reused content. Keep speed and quality together with a repeatable format:

  • Have a template. A fixed intro, section structure and outro for "news in my niche" or "first look" videos means you are not designing from scratch.
  • Script the first 30 seconds carefully and outline the rest. The script generator can produce a structured outline in minutes.
  • Prepare thumbnail templates in your channel style so only the image and text change. The thumbnail ideas tool helps with the concept.
  • Batch evergreen work in advance so you have time free when a trend appears.

Optimizing a trending video

Title

Put the trending keyword near the start of the title, then add your angle: a benefit, a comparison or a result. "New Photo Editing Feature: 5 Tricks Worth Learning" works better than a title that hides the topic. Generate variations with the title generator and pick the clearest.

Description

Use the keyword naturally in the first two sentences, then summarise what the viewer will learn. Add chapters with timestamps. The description generator creates a draft you can edit.

Tags and hashtags

Tags play a limited role in discovery, according to YouTube, but they help with common misspellings of new terms — which are frequent with trends. Add up to three relevant hashtags; the hashtag generator suggests options.

Timing

Publish as soon as the video is genuinely ready. Promote it in a community post, pin a comment that links to a related evergreen video, and add it to a relevant playlist.

Before publishing, run your title, description and tags through the YouTube SEO analyzer to catch missing keywords or weak metadata.

Policy lines not to cross

Trend-chasing creates specific risks. Stay clear of these:

  • Misleading metadata. YouTube's spam, deceptive practices and scams policy prohibits titles, thumbnails or descriptions that trick viewers about what the video contains. Never use a trending keyword that the video does not cover.
  • Keyword stuffing. Long lists of unrelated trending terms in descriptions or tags are treated as spam.
  • Reused content. Re-uploading others' clips about a trend without significant original commentary can make a channel ineligible for monetization.
  • Sensitive events. Tragedies and crises are covered by advertiser-friendly guidelines and specific policies. Exploiting them for views is likely to limit ads and harm trust.
  • Misinformation. On medical, election and other sensitive topics, YouTube's misinformation policies apply. Cite authoritative sources.

Building a weekly trend system

A simple routine is more effective than occasional deep dives:

  1. Monday (20 minutes): check Google Trends rising queries for your three main topics on YouTube Search, autocomplete for your core keywords, and the Studio Research tab.
  2. Log every candidate in a spreadsheet with the date, trend type, search evidence and fit score (1–5).
  3. Choose one high-fit rising trend or seasonal topic to film that week, alongside your planned evergreen content.
  4. Review after 28 days: compare views, average view duration and subscribers gained with your channel average. Keep doing the kinds of trends that worked.

Plan seasonal topics well in advance in a content calendar. Our guide to creating a YouTube content calendar shows how to schedule them.

Over a few months, this log becomes your most valuable research asset: a record of which kinds of trends your audience responds to. That lets you move faster and with more confidence each time a new one appears.

Trending keywords versus evergreen keywords

Trends are a supplement, not a strategy. Channels that grow steadily usually build most of their library around evergreen searches — questions people ask every month — and use trends to reach new viewers. A reasonable balance for many channels is roughly one trend-driven video for every three or four evergreen ones, adjusted based on what your analytics show.

When a trend matures into a lasting topic, update your early video, create a comprehensive guide, and link them together in a playlist. That is how a short-lived spike turns into a long-term source of traffic.

A worked example

Suppose you run a home-cooking channel. In your Monday check, Google Trends on YouTube Search shows a rising related query for a particular kitchen appliance recipe style, and autocomplete now suggests "... for beginners" and "... mistakes" variations that were not there last month. The YouTube results page for the main term is dominated by a few large channels, but the "mistakes" variation has only older, low-view videos.

That is a clear opportunity. You film "7 Beginner Mistakes With [the trend], and How to Fix Them," put the long-tail keyword near the start of the title, write chapters for each mistake, and link to your existing beginner recipe playlist in the pinned comment. Because the video answers a specific question with lasting value, it keeps earning search views after the initial wave passes — which is exactly what you want from a rising trend.

If you had instead chased the head term against the large channels, your video would likely have been buried on page three. Long-tail variations of a trend are usually where smaller channels win.

Measuring whether trend videos are worth it

After each trend video has been live for 28 days, compare it with your channel averages in YouTube Studio. Look at five numbers: views, impressions click-through rate, average view duration, subscribers gained, and the share of viewers who went on to watch another of your videos. A trend video that gets many views but few subscribers and almost no follow-on viewing brought in tourists. One with fewer views but strong subscriber gain and follow-on viewing brought in future regulars — that is the kind to repeat.

Also check where the views came from. If most arrived through YouTube search, the keyword choice worked and the video will likely keep collecting traffic. If most came from browse or suggested, the packaging and timing did the work. Both are useful, but they tell you different things about what to improve next time.

Tools checklist

  • Google Trends (YouTube Search filter) — rising and breakout queries.
  • YouTube autocomplete — real-time long-tail variations.
  • YouTube Studio Research tab — content gaps and your audience's searches.
  • VidSEOKit keyword tool — related keyword ideas from a seed.
  • Tag extractor — metadata used by ranking videos.
  • SEO analyzer — a final check before publishing.

None of these tools replaces judgement. They show you where demand is moving; your knowledge of your audience decides which trends are worth your time.

Start small: one check a week, one trend video a month, and an honest review of the results. Within a quarter you will know exactly which kinds of trends are worth chasing for your channel, and which are just noise.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best free tool to find trending YouTube keywords?

Google Trends with the search type set to YouTube Search, combined with YouTube autocomplete and the Studio Research tab.

Should I make videos on every trend?

No. Only chase trends that fit your channel's audience, or you may attract viewers who never return.

Can I add trending keywords my video doesn't cover?

No. Misleading metadata breaches YouTube's spam and deceptive practices policy.
